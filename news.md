## 26.09 New papers on stochastic extension of fixed-point and minimax acceleration

Our paper <q><a href="https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/loc/atlanta/poster/153100">Direct Acceleration of Stochastic Root-Finding Without Variance Reduction and Regularization</a></q> has been accepted at NeurIPS 2026.
We followed up on it in a more recent paper <q><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36033">Dual-Anchor Acceleration Is Near-Optimal for Stochastic Monotone Root-Finding</a></q>, where we eventually improved the algorithm's complexity to the near-optimal level.

Traditionally, deterministic acceleration does not extend easily to stochastic optimization.
We discover that for fixed-point and minimax problems, certain acceleration doesn't, but another one does!

## 26.09 New papers on monotone inclusions/root-finding problems

Two new papers have been announced: <q><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26581">Polyak-Type Extragradient Methods for Monotone Root-Finding Problems</a></q> and <q><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30212">Anchored Extra-Proximal Methods: Optimal Higher-Order Methods for Monotone Inclusion Problems</a></q>.

Each work connects between two distinct literatures. The first paper connects (stochastic) Polyak step-sizes from convex optimization with minimax/monotone inclusion problems.
The second paper connects first-order anchor acceleration for minimax/monotone inclusion problems with the literature on higher-order Newton/tensor algorithms.

## 26.06 New paper on Lyapunov analyses in optimization

Our new paper <q><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26077">Toward a Systematic Understanding and Interactive Search of Lyapunov-Style Proofs in Optimization</a></q> has been announced.

We characterize when and how a tight proof, i.e., a combination of inequalities, admits a translation into a Lyapunov-style proof in optimization.

Combining this with AI (coding agents) is a very promising direction. We have presented the preliminary results in a separate work <q><a href="https://openreview.net/pdf?id=q7TfzOgGnb">Peppy: An AI-Assisted Workflow for Tight Convergence Analysis of Optimization Algorithms</a></q> at <a href="https://ai4research-icml-workshop.github.io/">ICML 2026 AI4Research Workshop</a>.

## 24.05 New paper on minimax optimization and fixed-point problems

Our new paper <q><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13228">Optimal Acceleration for Minimax and Fixed-Point Problems is Not Unique</a></q> has been announced.

We discover novel algorithms for fixed-point problems (equivalent to proximal algorithms for monotone inclusion) achieving the exact optimal complexity.
We establish a new duality theory between fixed-point algorithms (the H-duality) and exhibit an analogous phenomenon in minimax optimization, together with the continuous-time analysis bridging between the two setups.

## 22.05 New paper on minimax optimization

Our new paper <q><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11093">Accelerated Minimax Algorithms Flock Together</a></q> has been announced. 

It establishes a new connection, the **<em>merging path property</em>**, among order-optimal algorithms for minimax optimization and order-optimal algorithms for fixed-point problems, which are all based on the mechanism of anchoring ($\approx$ Halpern iteration).

The paper is currently under revision at <i>SIAM Journal on Optimization</i>.
